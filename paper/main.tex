\begin{abstract}
\todo{rewrite once Sec.~I--VI are drafted; this is a scaffold that already
carries the final numbers}
Given one object mask in one camera at one instant, a static multi-camera rig
must produce the mask of that object in every other camera and every subsequent
frame, without camera calibration, category labels, or per-scene optimisation.
We study this task on MUVOD, the only public rig benchmark, and report two
results. First, a two-stage pipeline built on a promptable segmentation
foundation model reaches \OursBasic\ \JF\ (basic) and \OursComplete\ (complete),
against \XMemBasic/\XMemComplete\ for the published baseline --- a paired
improvement of \DeltaBasic\ points ($t=\tBasic$, 95\% CI $\ciBasic$,
winning in \winLoss\ scenes). Second, and more informative for the field, we
decompose that margin: \gainFoundation\ of it is attributable to the foundation
model inside an unchanged two-stage structure, and only \gainOurs\ to our own
design --- cross-view memory during tracking, raw frame-0 seeding, and
zoom-gated crop re-tracking. We then account for the \totalGap\ points that
remain. An oracle decomposition attributes \oracleSeed\ to seeding failures,
\oracleRef\ to reference-camera re-prediction, and \oracleOrdinary\ to ordinary
mask quality on pairs that never fail; a ground-truth-through-the-grid
measurement shows the tracker's \gridNow-cell mask grid costs only \lossNow\ of
those points. We further report five pre-registered attempts to repair
degenerate seeds --- example detection, dense correspondence, and three
geometry-lifting families --- all of which fail, and we identify the reason: on
this benchmark there are \nFzero\ pairs to fix against \nNzero\ to protect, an
\asymmetry\ asymmetry that any imperfect selector turns into a net loss.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Zoom-gated crop re-tracking}
\label{sec:crop}
\todo{Objects whose seed is below \(10^4\) px are re-tracked inside a crop
window $s=\mathrm{clamp}(\lceil \alpha \max(w,h)\rceil, s_{\min}, \min(H,W))$
resized to the model resolution, giving zoom $=1008/s$. A gate reverts a pair

below threshold, or when the object rides the window edge.}
\note{The gate thresholds were chosen on the same \nScenes\ scenes that are
scored. Sec.~\ref{sec:loso} bounds the resulting optimism instead of hiding

\label{sec:grid}
\todo{Table~\ref{tab:grid}. Passing ground truth through the tracker's own

\subsection{The structural reason}
\label{sec:asymmetry}
\todo{\textbf{This is the most transferable result in the paper.} There are
\nFzero\ pairs that need repair and \nNzero\ that must not be touched, an
\asymmetry\ asymmetry. A selector with anything short of near-perfect precision
on the majority class destroys more than it repairs: in our run, 10 of 12
repairs on the failing set succeeded and 0 broke, while 11 of 16 substitutions
on the sound set broke a working pair. Any future work on seed repair for rigs
must report precision on the \emph{sound} population, not recall on the failing
one.}
